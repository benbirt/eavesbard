# E12 — Illustration library (research)

**Status:** research done (2026-10-10); nothing built. Direction changed to official D&D art: see "Round 2" below. Goal: art that changes with the scene (6 settings × 3 intensities = 18 combinations), free to use in a public, non-commercial web app, with a coherent look.

## Recommendation

**Curate a fixed set of public-domain illustrations and bundle them with the site.**
- Choose 3–5 per setting and intensity, about 60–90 in all, mostly 18th–19th-century engravings and book illustrations. These cover every combination (examples below) and already look like fantasy art.
- Unify them with one visual treatment in CSS, for example a duotone tinted by intensity: warm amber for calm, deep blue for tense, crimson for combat. That way paintings and engravings from different sources still read as one set.
- Resize to WebP (about 150–250 KB each, 10–20 MB in all) and serve them from our own site, which is reliable, quick and works offline from cache.
- Keep a manifest (file, title, artist, date, source page, licence) and generate a credits page from it, alongside Tabletop Audio's attribution.
- Curation needs a person's taste: a script can pull candidates per combination from Commons and the museum APIs into a contact sheet to choose from.

**Add game-icons.net** for small setting and intensity icons in the UI (CC BY 3.0, thousands of fantasy icons, credit each author).

### Why not D&D or Pathfinder art

- **Wizards of the Coast:** its Fan Content Policy allows its art in free fan content with a set notice, and forbids its logos. But there's no clean source of images (scanning books isn't covered), Eavesbard isn't D&D-specific, and the policy can change.
- **Paizo:** its Community Use Policy covers only art Paizo has released for it (its blog, iconic characters, covers), for non-commercial use, and forbids extracting art from books.

## Practical notes

- **Check each image's status:** public domain worldwide needs the artist dead 70+ years (Doré 1883, Piranesi 1778, Pyle 1911, Bauer 1918, Rackham 1939). Commons marks this on each file.
- **Hotlinking** is possible but not the plan:
  - The Art Institute of Chicago explicitly allows it (images send `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`; it asks for an `AIC-User-Agent` header and warns images can be unpublished).
  - Wikimedia allows it but recommends downloading.
- **APIs work from a browser**, all with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`: Commons, Openverse (filter `license=cc0,pdm`), the Art Institute (`is_public_domain`) and the Met (`/v1.1/search`; the old `/v1/search` was retired on 2026-10-01). Useful for a curation script.
- **Content:** museum and book art includes nudity and gore; curate by hand rather than search live.
- **Credits:** public domain needs no credit, but credit anyway (title, artist, source); game-icons.net requires it.

## Round 2: official D&D art (2026-10-10)

The public-domain set was the wrong style; the target is modern D&D book art.

**Licence.** Wizards of the Coast's [Fan Content Policy](https://company.wizards.com/en/legal/fancontentpolicy) covers "artwork… and other materials published by Wizards" in free fan content (FAQ: "use Wizards' art? Yes! We love it!"). Conditions that apply to Eavesbard:
- It stays free: no payment, sign-up or survey to use it.
- It shows the notice: "Eavesbard is unofficial Fan Content permitted under the Fan Content Policy. Not approved/endorsed by Wizards. Portions of the materials used are property of Wizards of the Coast. ©Wizards of the Coast LLC."
- It uses no Wizards logos or trademarks, and removes no notices already in the art.
- It doesn't incorporate the art into a game. Eavesbard is a tool for running one, which we read as fine.
- Wizards can change the policy or ask for content to be removed.

**Which art counts.** Only art from books Wizards itself published. Excluded:
- licensed books from other publishers (*Heroes' Feast*, DK's *Legend of Drizzt Visual Dictionary*);
- film tie-ins (*Honor Among Thieves*);
- video-game art (for example *Baldur's Gate 3* concept art, which is Larian's);
- comics.

**Sources ruled out:**
- **Scryfall** (Magic card art): its terms limit images mainly to Magic software and forbid colour-shifting.
- **D&D Beyond:** its terms forbid automated access.
- **Wizards' old press-assets page:** gone (404).

**Mistake and correction.** The first shortlist was gathered from the Forgotten Realms Wiki (Fandom) by script. Fandom's terms forbid robots, scrapers or other automated means and scraping content without written permission, and forbid forging headers (the thumbnail requests sent Fandom's own site as the referrer). The scraped data and thumbnails were deleted and removed from the repo, and Fandom and D&D Beyond aren't to be accessed automatically. The art itself is still usable under Wizards' policy; final images are to be saved by hand from the art's pages.

**Shortlist.** One image per setting and intensity, all from Wizards-published books, recorded as facts and page links only: `dnd/picks.json`. Reviewed with thumbs up or down in `dnd/review.html`.
