# E13 — The classifier on real play (Bardo transcripts)

**Status:** Bardo: labels, harness and two rounds done (2026-10-11). Critical Role (CRD3): ten episodes labelled; first replay running.

## Method

The replay runs the real app (`//src:site`) in headless Chrome on the GPU, with `?replay` in its address (`src/replay.ts`): instead of the microphone, each caption line goes to the listener when its speech would have finished, stamped with its start time. Everything after that is the app's own code, unchanged: the director's 15-second scene checks over the transcript buffer, the embeddings blended with Gemma 4 E2B, the state machine and its minimum times, and the track picks. The page's clock is Playwright's: time flows as usual, so the models and their workers run normally, but between lines the clock jumps ahead to the next line's time, firing the director's timer on the way, and each check finishes before the replay goes on. An episode of half an hour replays in about a minute. Tracks are served as silence, so nothing is fetched from Tabletop Audio.

Each episode starts from its first labelled scene, set by hand as the scene card would, so the test is of changes rather than the opening guess. The results (`e13/results/`) keep every scene check, decision and track pick with its time into the episode, but no transcript text.

Scoring (`e13/score.mjs`) takes the scene the app was in after each check, and separately what the classifier said before the state machine. Checks within a minute of a labelled intensity change, or three minutes of a setting change, aren't counted as steady, since lag there is by design; fights are scored separately (checks to call combat after a fight's labelled start, with up to two minutes early counting as on time; and time to leave it after the end).

    bazel run //experiments/e13:replay -- --name run1 [--set crd3] [--episodes 1,2]
    bazel run //experiments/e13:score -- experiments/e13/results/run1.json

On NixOS, run these inside `nix develop` (flake.nix). Headless Chrome on Linux falls back to SwiftShader, a CPU renderer, unless told to use Vulkan; the harness passes the flags, and stops if it still gets a software adapter. A normal Chrome window needs no flags: on the same machine it gets the GPU by itself.

## Data

The "Call of the Wild" set from Bardo (Padovani, Ferreira and Lelis, AIIDE 2017): 9 episodes of a YouTube D&D game (a DM and three players, a homebrew campaign), about 4.6 hours and 6,100 lines, in https://github.com/lucasnfe/bardo-composer under `data/dnd/epN.csv`. Each line is a YouTube auto-caption (`start`, `duration`, `text`) with a hand label, valence and arousal, 0 or 1: agitated (0, 1), suspenseful (0, 0), calm (1, 0) and happy (1, 1, very rare). The repo is MIT, but the words are the show's: the harness downloads them at run time, and nothing here quotes them.

## Labels

`e13/labels.json` holds reference labels written by Claude from reading every caption: per episode, stretches with a start time, every acceptable intensity and setting (the first is the best fit; more than one means a judgement call) and a short note in our own words. They are a reference, not ground truth.

- **Bardo's agitated isn't the same as combat.** In episode 1 it also covers shooting an elk on a hunt, banter after a fight, and running back to find a raided camp; those are labelled tense or calm here. In later episodes the two match closely.
- **Agreement with Bardo**, mapping agitated → combat, suspenseful → tense, calm and happy → calm: 97% of lines get a Bardo label that our labels accept (episode 1: 87%; the others 98–100%). The largest differences are agitated lines labelled tense (94) or calm (65). This isn't an independent check: stretch boundaries mostly follow Bardo's label changes.
- **18 fights** (stretches whose best label is combat), plus 5 brief kills marked "combat or tense". Episode 7 has none.
- **Settings:** mostly wilderness (episodes 1, 7, 8, 9) and town (2, 3, 4), with the mansion as interior (5, 6) and its cellar of cages as interior or dungeon (5). Tavern appears only as an alternative, while one character is inside one. Travel appears once, briefly, on a cart. No stretch is tavern, travel or dungeon alone.
- Settings change faster than the app allows (a setting lasts at least three minutes, DESIGN.md 7.6), in episode 2 especially (woods, farms, houses); scoring should allow for that.

## Round 1: held out, nothing tuned (2026-10-11)

All 9 episodes, the app as it is (`results/run1.json`), on an RTX 5080 (Linux, Chrome 155): 991 scene checks, every one with Gemma, at a median 0.46 s per check (embeddings and Gemma's three or four questions together; on the Apple M5, Gemma alone took 0.45–0.9 s, E9).

**A leak, found and fixed first.** The first full run lost Gemma partway through episode 3: after about 260 checks the GPU ran out of memory (`VK_ERROR_OUT_OF_DEVICE_MEMORY` in ONNX Runtime), and every later question failed. The app carried on with the embeddings alone and said nothing. The LLM worker read each pass's logits but never freed the rest of what the pass returned, the key-value cache included, which stays on the GPU. It now frees it (`src/llm/llm-worker.ts`), and GPU memory stayed flat through all 991 checks. A real session would have hit this after about an hour, and the cost was large: over the same 9 episodes, the run with the leak (Gemma answering 26% of checks) scored 59% on intensity and 47% on setting, with combat 34% of the time in calm or tense play (29 false starts); the fixed run below scores 71%, 68% and 10% (3). The timeline now also says when Gemma stops answering, and when it recovers (`src/llm/scene-llm.ts`).

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Intensity, away from changes | 71% of 787 checks (classifier alone 69%) |
| Setting, away from changes | 68% of 854 checks (classifier alone 67%) |
| Fights called | 17 of 18, median 18 s after the labelled start |
| Fights left | 17 of 17, median 14 s after the labelled end |

By episode (intensity, setting): 1: 57%, 91%; 2: 70%, 58%; 3: 82%, 19%; 4: 67%, 8%; 5: 100%, 72%; 6: 83%, 85%; 7: 39%, 100%; 8: 64%, 80%; 9: 68%, 100%.

- **Fights are the strength.** All but one were called, most within a check or two; the miss is a 48-second scuffle (episode 4, 8:33). The slow ones are fights that build up: episode 1's raid on the camp (called after 2.5 minutes) and episode 9's archers (48 s). Leaving combat is quick, apart from episode 9 (6.7 minutes, as the party sneaks up on the giants) and episode 6 (2 minutes).
- **Calm play is heard as tense.** Of 185 steady calm checks, 148 were tense. This is the largest error: planning, recaps and in-character talk about danger (episode 7, the home tree, is 39%). Tense play is rarely heard as calm (1 check).
- **Combat stretches heard as tense** (50 checks) are mostly the quiet turns of long fights: planning a move, an NPC talking.
- **Town is the weak setting.** Labelled town, the app chose tavern (147 decisions), wilderness (103) and town only 27 times. Town here is mostly night-time skulking round a trading post with a tavern (episode 3) and a mansion's grounds and woods (episode 4), so both answers are understandable, but town itself is almost never heard. Interior is heard well (170 of 269), sometimes as dungeon (49) or tavern (43); wilderness sometimes as dungeon (61).

Round 1 is "not great" on calm and on town, so the next step, as planned, is to split by episode, tune on about four and score on the other five.

## Round 2: tuning on four episodes, scored on five (2026-10-11)

Tuned on episodes 1, 3, 6 and 7 (calm-heavy wilderness, the town and tavern episode, the mansion, and the episode without fights); held out 2, 4, 5, 8 and 9. Both halves have town, calm play and fights. Each candidate replayed the four tuning episodes through the app (`results/tuneA–F.json`). The labels were not changed.

**Where the errors came from**, from each check's reason (round 1, tuning episodes): on calm stretches Gemma said tense in about 110 of 153 checks, and when it said calm the result was nearly always calm; the embeddings were seldom right either (24). On town, Gemma said tavern in about 110 of 130 checks; the embeddings scattered, often to dungeon.

| Candidate (tuning episodes) | Intensity | Setting | Calm right |
|---|---|---|---|
| Round 1 | 67% | 72% | 25 of 127 |
| A: Gemma's calm, tense, tavern and town reworded | 70% | 58% | 38 |
| B: only calm and tense reworded ("no danger in the scene right now", planning, looting, recapping) | 70% | 72% | 38 |
| C: B, and the embeddings' table-talk probability counted as calm in the blend | **74%** | 72% | 56 |
| D: C without "keep that answer" in Gemma's intensity question | 73% | 72% | 59 (fights called later) |
| E: C with three more calm descriptions for the embeddings | 73% | 72% | 55 |
| F: C with Gemma's intensity weight 0.4 instead of 0.5 | 67% | 72% | 43 (more false combat) |

Rewording the settings moved the mansion to dungeon and left town as tavern: Gemma's setting answers swing on small wording changes.

**Held out, C against round 1** (`results/heldoutC.json`): intensity 73% against 74%, setting 64% both, fights unchanged (12 of 13 called, median 13 s; all left, median 10 s). Calm improved a little (11 of 58 checks against 7), but tense heard as calm went from 1 to 9 checks and combat heard as calm from 5 to 15, the worst kind of mistake for the music. The tuning episodes were calm-heavy and the held-out ones mostly tense and combat, so C's trade paid off on one and not the other. **Not adopted**: the app is unchanged.

What this suggests:
- Calm against tense is partly a judgement call in these labels (planning a raid, a recap of a massacre, talk of an army coming), and 9 episodes of one group are too few to tune it without trading away fights. More varied labelled play would help more than more tuning here.
- Town is barely heard, by either model, in night-time skulking round buildings. The scenes are ambiguous, and a label "town or tavern" would be defensible for more of episode 3; the labels stay as they are, but the result should be read with that in mind.
- Fights are already good on real play, and any change should be checked against them first.

## A second set: Critical Role (CRD3)

Bardo's 9 episodes are one group, 4.6 hours, with few calm or town stretches to tune on. CRD3 (Rameshkumar and Bailey, ACL 2020, https://github.com/RevanthRameshkumar/CRD3; CC BY-SA 4.0) has 159 Critical Role episodes as human transcripts, with the Critical Role Wiki's summary of each episode, split into sentences that the authors aligned to stretches of the transcript. Ten episodes were chosen for variety, by their wiki summaries: C1E014 (shopping, a council), C1E024 (errands; a feast with enemies), C1E044 (a frozen loch and a sunken tomb), C1E065 (a city and its bazaar), C2E002 (an investigation in a town), C2E003 (zombies; an island), C2E005 (the road; a gnoll raid), C2E006 (a gnoll mine), C2E035 (a wharf at night; a fight aboard a ship), C2E036 (at sea; harpies; a reef).

- **Timing.** The transcripts have no times, so `e13/crd3.mjs` gives each word 0.4 s (150 words a minute) and cuts each speaker's turn into segments of up to 15 words, about what speech-to-text delivers; speaker names are dropped, as the app never has them. The same clock is used for labelling and replay. The text is clean, so this set is kinder to the classifier than real speech recognition.
- **Labels** (`e13/labels-crd3.json`, CC BY-SA 4.0 like their source), by Claude: from the wiki synopsis and its aligned summaries as the outline, a minute-by-minute view of the transcript (opening words, and counts of words such as initiative, damage, stealth), and the full transcript wherever a boundary needed placing. Pre-shows, announcements, recaps, breaks and outros are marked "skip" and not scored, though the replay passes through breaks, as the app would hear them.
- **What's in it:** 140 stretches, about 34 hours scored: calm 19 hours, tense 8, combat 7, in 9 fights (Critical Role's fights are long). Settings: town 9.6 hours, interior 7.6, dungeon 5, travel 4.3, wilderness 3.7, tavern 3.7.
